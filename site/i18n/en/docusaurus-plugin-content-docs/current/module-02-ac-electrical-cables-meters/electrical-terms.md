# Chapter: Electrical Terms

**Module 2:** AC & Electrical Cables, Single Range Meters
**Study No.:** 1.2.13 – 1.2.21

---

## 1. Introduction

Before doing any electronics or electrical work, it's necessary to understand a few basic terms. These terms come up again and again in every circuit, every meter reading, and every calculation. In this chapter we'll go through all these fundamental electrical terms.

---

## 2. Electric Charge

- Every substance is made up of tiny particles — Proton (positive charge), Electron (negative charge), Neutron (no charge).
- The unit of **Charge** is the **Coulomb (C)**.
- Charge of one electron = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C

---

## 3. Electric Current

- The continuous flow of electrons in a conductor is called **Current**.
- **Formula:** I = Q / t (I = current, Q = charge, t = time)
- **Unit:** Ampere (A)
- **Measuring instrument:** Ammeter

---

## 4. Voltage / EMF

- The difference in electrical pressure (potential difference) between two points, which drives electrons to flow, is called **Voltage**.
- **Unit:** Volt (V)
- **Measuring instrument:** Voltmeter
- **EMF (Electromotive Force)** is the total voltage of a source (battery/generator) without any load connected.

---

## 5. Resistance

- The opposition a material offers to the flow of current is called **Resistance**.
- **Unit:** Ohm (Ω)
- **Measuring instrument:** Ohmmeter
- Resistance depends on the material, its length, cross-section area, and temperature.

---

## 6. Ohm's Law

At constant temperature, the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it.

```
V = I × R

where:
V = Voltage (Volt)
I = Current (Ampere)
R = Resistance (Ohm)
```

**Example:** If a circuit has R = 100Ω and V = 10V, what is the current?
```
I = V / R = 10 / 100 = 0.1 A
```

---

## 7. Power

- The rate of doing work, i.e. the rate at which electrical energy is used.
- **Formula:** P = V × I
- **Unit:** Watt (W)

---

## 8. Energy

- Power used over a certain time is called **Energy**.
- **Formula:** E = P × t
- **Unit:** Watt-hour, or the "unit" (kWh) used on household electricity bills.

---

## 9. AC and DC

| Property | DC (Direct Current) | AC (Alternating Current) |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Flows in one direction | Direction keeps reversing |
| Example | Battery, cell | Household mains supply |
| Waveform | Straight line | Sine wave |

---

## 10. Frequency

- The number of complete cycles an AC supply completes in one second is called **Frequency**.
- **Unit:** Hertz (Hz)
- Frequency of household mains supply in India: **50 Hz**

---

## 11. Key Points

- Current (I) = in Amperes, Voltage (V) = in Volts, Resistance (R) = in Ohms
- V = I × R (Ohm's Law)
- P = V × I (Power)
- AC frequency in India = 50 Hz
