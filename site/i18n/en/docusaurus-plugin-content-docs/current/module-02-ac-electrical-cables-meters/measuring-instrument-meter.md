# Chapter: Measuring Instrument Meter

**Module 2:** AC & Electrical Cables, Single Range Meters
**Study No.:** 1.2.22 – 1.2.27

---

## 1. Introduction

In the last chapter we learned about voltage, current, and resistance. But to actually measure these quantities, we need a **Meter**. In this chapter we'll learn about the main measuring instruments, their types, and how to connect them in a circuit.

---

## 2. Main Measuring Instruments

| Instrument | Measures | Unit | Circuit connection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ammeter | Current | Ampere (A) | In Series |
| Voltmeter | Voltage | Volt (V) | In Parallel |
| Ohmmeter | Resistance | Ohm (Ω) | Power off, directly on the component |
| Multimeter | All three (V, I, R) | All | Depending on the selected function |
| Wattmeter | Power | Watt (W) | Both voltage coil and current coil |

---

## 3. Circuit Connection of Ammeter and Voltmeter

An ammeter is always connected in **Series** — because the current must pass through it. A voltmeter is always connected in **Parallel** — because it needs to measure the pressure difference between two points.

## 4. Reading an Analog Meter

An analog meter has a **Pointer** that moves across a **Scale**.

**Steps to take a reading:**
1. Select the meter's **Range** — choose a range slightly higher than the expected value.
2. Read the scale value where the pointer settles.
3. **Least Count** = (difference between two main markings) / (number of small divisions between them)
4. Keep your eye directly in front of the scale (to avoid parallax error).

**Figure 2:** Diagram explaining the pointer and least count on an analog meter dial (add a screenshot to the blog post)

---

## 5. Meter Accuracy & Care

- Always use the meter on the **correct range** — selecting too small a range can damage the meter.
- A voltmeter's resistance should be very **high**, so it doesn't affect the circuit's current.
- An ammeter's resistance should be very **low**, so it doesn't affect the circuit's voltage.
- Digital multimeters (DMM) are more commonly used today — more accurate, easier to read.

---

## 6. Key Points

- Ammeter = in series, Voltmeter = in parallel
- Ammeter resistance should be low, voltmeter resistance should be high
- You can't take an accurate reading without understanding least count
- Selecting the wrong range can damage the meter
