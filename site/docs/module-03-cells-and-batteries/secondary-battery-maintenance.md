# Chapter: Secondary Battery – Types of Charge, Discharge and Maintenance

**Module:** Cells and Batteries — Electronic Mechanic Trade
**Study No.:** 1.3.29 – 1.3.34

---

## 1. Introduction

A secondary (rechargeable) battery is only as good as the way it's charged and maintained. Charging it wrong, or neglecting maintenance, shortens its life drastically. In this chapter we'll learn the common charging methods, how to judge a battery's state of charge, and the maintenance practices used to keep secondary batteries — especially lead-acid batteries — healthy.

---

## 2. Methods of Charging

| Method | How it works | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Constant Current (CC) charging** | Charger holds current fixed, voltage rises as battery charges | Ni-Cd, some lead-acid charging stages |
| **Constant Voltage (CV) charging** | Charger holds voltage fixed, current tapers off as battery fills up | Lead-acid, Li-ion final charging stage |
| **Trickle / Float charging** | Very small continuous current keeps a fully-charged battery topped up | Standby batteries (UPS, emergency lighting) that sit fully charged most of the time |
| **Fast charging** | High current for quick charging, needs careful control to avoid overheating | Modern Li-ion battery chargers |

Most modern chargers (e.g. for lead-acid or Li-ion) combine methods — Constant Current first, then switching to Constant Voltage as the battery nears full charge.

---

## 3. Checking State of Charge

- **Voltage check:** A resting (no load, no charge) lead-acid cell reads about 2.1V when fully charged, dropping toward 1.75V when discharged. For a 12V battery, a healthy resting voltage is around 12.6V; below 12V generally means it needs charging.
- **Specific Gravity (SG) check (lead-acid only):** Using a **hydrometer** to measure the density of the electrolyte. A fully charged cell reads about 1.260–1.280 SG; a discharged cell drops to about 1.100–1.150 SG.
- **Load test:** Applying a known load and observing how the voltage holds up — a healthy battery holds its voltage under load; a weak battery's voltage collapses quickly.

---

## 4. Discharge Characteristics

- A battery's usable capacity depends on its **discharge rate** — discharging faster (higher current, higher "C-rating") delivers less total energy than discharging slowly.
- **Deep discharge** (draining a battery far below its rated cutoff voltage) damages most battery chemistries and shortens life — lead-acid and Li-ion batteries in particular should not be regularly discharged below their recommended cutoff.
- Batteries should generally not be left in a discharged state for long periods — this can cause sulfation (lead-acid) or permanent capacity loss (Li-ion, Ni-Cd).

---

## 5. Maintenance Practices (Lead-Acid Batteries)

- **Check electrolyte level** regularly (for non-sealed/vented batteries) — top up with **distilled water only**, never tap water or acid, when the level is low.
- **Check specific gravity** with a hydrometer periodically to judge state of charge and detect a weak cell.
- **Keep terminals clean** — corrosion (white/greenish buildup) increases resistance; clean with a wire brush and a baking-soda-and-water solution, then apply a thin coat of petroleum jelly to prevent recurrence.
- **Keep the battery case dry and clean** — a dirty, damp surface can create a slow discharge path between terminals.
- **Equalizing charge:** periodically giving a controlled overcharge to a lead-acid battery bank to equalize the charge level across all cells and reduce sulfation — done per manufacturer's schedule, not routinely.
- **Avoid deep discharge and prolonged storage in a discharged state.**
- **Ensure proper ventilation** — charging lead-acid batteries releases hydrogen gas, which is explosive in an enclosed space.

---

## 6. Safety Precautions

- Always wear **eye protection and gloves** when handling battery electrolyte (acid) — it is corrosive.
- Never create a spark or open flame near a charging or recently-charged battery, due to hydrogen gas buildup.
- Disconnect the negative terminal first when removing a battery, and connect it last when installing, to reduce the risk of short-circuit sparks.
- Never short-circuit the terminals — this can cause the battery to overheat, vent gas violently, or even explode.

---

## 7. Key Points to Remember

- Constant Current and Constant Voltage are the two basic charging methods; most chargers use both in stages
- Specific gravity (measured with a hydrometer) is the most reliable way to check a lead-acid cell's state of charge
- Deep discharge and prolonged storage while discharged both shorten battery life
- Regular electrolyte level checks, terminal cleaning, and proper ventilation are the core maintenance tasks for lead-acid batteries
- Hydrogen gas released during charging is explosive — always charge in a ventilated area, away from sparks
