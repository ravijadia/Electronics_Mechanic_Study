# Chapter: Types of Measuring Instruments, Equipment, Uses and Features

**Module:** AC/DC Measuring Instruments, Basic Workshop Practice — Electronic Mechanic Trade
**Study No.:** 1.4.35 & 1.4.36

---

## 1. Introduction

A technician's work depends on trustworthy measurements — of voltage, current, resistance, and waveforms. Different jobs need different instruments, and each instrument has its own accuracy, features, and correct way of being used. This chapter covers how measuring instruments are classified and the common types you'll use on the job.

---

## 2. Classification of Measuring Instruments

| Category | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Indicating instrument** | Shows the reading at the moment of measurement (pointer or digital display); doesn't record it | Ammeter, Voltmeter, Multimeter |
| **Recording instrument** | Continuously records the measured quantity over time, usually on paper or digitally | Chart recorder, data logger |
| **Integrating instrument** | Totals up a quantity over time | Energy meter (kWh meter) |

Instruments are also classified by how they display a reading:

- **Analog instrument:** Uses a moving pointer over a scale.
- **Digital instrument:** Shows the reading as a numeric display — generally more accurate and easier to read.

---

## 3. Analog Meter Movements

| Type | Working principle | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| **PMMC (Permanent Magnet Moving Coil)** | A coil carrying current sits in a permanent magnet's field; the resulting force moves the pointer, proportional to current | DC only (needs a rectifier to measure AC) |
| **Moving Iron (MI)** | Current in a coil magnetizes a soft-iron vane, which moves against a spring | Both AC and DC |
| **Dynamometer type** | Uses interaction between a fixed coil and a moving coil, both carrying current | AC and DC, often used in wattmeters |

PMMC meters are generally more sensitive and accurate for DC; Moving Iron meters are simpler and rugged, commonly used for AC panel meters.

---

## 4. Key Features and Terms

- **Accuracy:** How close a reading is to the true value, usually stated as a percentage of full-scale reading (e.g. ±2% FSD).
- **Sensitivity:** The smallest change in the measured quantity the instrument can detect.
- **Resolution:** The smallest increment a digital instrument can display.
- **Linearity:** How evenly the scale/reading tracks the actual quantity across its full range.
- **Range:** The minimum to maximum value the instrument can measure on a given setting.
- **True RMS vs Average-responding:** Basic analog/digital meters measure AC by responding to the average value and scaling it to show an equivalent RMS reading — accurate only for pure sine waves. A **True RMS** meter calculates the actual RMS value regardless of waveform shape, needed for accurate readings on distorted or non-sinusoidal AC (e.g. from variable-speed drives, dimmer circuits).

---

## 5. Common Instruments Used in the Workshop

- **Multimeter (Analog or Digital):** Measures voltage, current, and resistance; the most-used general-purpose instrument.
- **Clamp meter:** Measures current without breaking the circuit, by clamping around a conductor.
- **Oscilloscope (CRO/DSO):** Displays how a voltage signal varies with time — essential for viewing waveforms, covered in detail in the next chapter.
- **Function generator:** Produces test signals (sine, square, triangle waves) of adjustable frequency and amplitude, used to test circuits.
- **Wattmeter:** Measures electrical power directly.
- **Energy meter:** Totals energy consumed over time (kWh), used for billing.

---

## 6. Key Points to Remember

- Instruments are classified as indicating, recording, or integrating
- PMMC meters work on DC only; Moving Iron meters work on both AC and DC
- Digital instruments are generally more accurate and easier to read than analog
- True RMS meters are needed for accurate readings on non-sinusoidal AC waveforms
- Always check an instrument's range and accuracy rating before relying on its reading
